The worst app idea ever pitched, in one sentence: a product that sounds real, a founder who sounds certain, and a function nobody on earth needs.

## The entry

> Introducing UmbrellaAlert: an app that texts you every morning it is NOT raining, so you never carry an umbrella by mistake, for people terrified of being prepared for the wrong weather, at just $4.99 a month.

209 characters, one line, plain ASCII — inside the 1–240 character gate.

## Why this line

Written against the judge's brief, in the order it weighs things:

1. **Names a specific app and what it uselessly does** — UmbrellaAlert texts you on every dry morning, so you never carry an umbrella by mistake.
2. **Funny through absurd logic and deadpan confidence** — a weather app that is only useful on the days you do not need it, solving the non-problem of being over-prepared, for people "terrified of being prepared for the wrong weather" — and it charges $4.99 a month for the privilege, so the uselessness is not just free noise but a paid subscription to being told it is not raining.
3. **Sounds like a real pitch** — a launch announcement with a product name, a clear function, a target audience, and a price tag.
4. **Uses the 240 characters well** — one clean sentence, no padding, no second idea crowding it.
5. **Leaves a vivid, specific picture** — your phone buzzing on a sunny morning to announce that it is not raining.
